“Bonjour?”
“ Bonjour!”
“Do you have a daycare?”“ yes! ”

“ Do you have a place for my son? He’s 3 years old. ”

“ Yes.”

“Ok! We just moved to this city. My son can’t speak English nor French, and doesn’t like daycare, so he will cry for sure... ”

“ It’s ok, does he pee himself? Or needs a diaper? ”

“Himself.”

“Any allergy?”

“No. And his name is David.”

“ Ok! He can come tomorrow.”

This is our first meeting 22 years ago,with Paul and his wife Marie, at their house’s  front door, where hanging a small, colorful board written “Garderie en milieu familliale”.
<!--more-->

Place shortage in daycare is a permanent problem in this city. After such a hard search here and there, during the whole beginning 2 months of landing in this city, from my home on the other side of the earth, I was exhausted but finally ensured our settlement completely.

Then it was David’s turn to challenge his daily life in this homecare, with mostly white kids around, which was totally different from his life in China. Bringing this huge change to my little boy, I felt I was cruel and cold, full of worries and guilt every day. He started almost every morning by crying, but when left in the afternoon, he was quite happy. One morning after 2 months, he stopped crying, then a few days later, he spoke French with the couple and his friends. At the end of that summer, David was invited by a girl in the homecare for her birthday party at her home. We had never expected this big progress within just 3 months! How amazing! Thanks to Paul and Marie, who did all their best to take care of a boy who spoke only mandarin at the beginning, and to communicate hardly with us- parents who spoke poor English and French. They were so qualified that easily got David fitting into his first social group, all like the other local kids.

Paul is an artist, a painter, and also a musician, playing guitar and piano. He gives private courses in painting and music, sometimes busy, sometimes so free, then he helps Marie, to run the homecare. 

We were special clients to them, as a family from China, which was not so popular,neither so welcome yet in this city, at least not familiar to a quebecor family. But luckily for us, they have an open mind for people of different races. As an artist, Paul was the same curious and interested in Chinese culture as us in theirs. Marie has a super warm heart, always trying to help us. Friendship is growing.

Besides our daily transfering my son, we visited each other more and more, we went together to downtown, museums, parks, etc. Some beautiful moments built up into my mind forever: when we had dinner the first time at my, and their home, when Paul held David in his arms and slept on the sofa, when we passed halloween with Paul’s family, going out for a “trick or treat”, Paul did a lovely makeup on David… They kept helping us whenever we had difficulties, explaining the local services, searching for an apartment for us, getting us some necessities as much as they could: tables, chairs, floor lamps, etc.. Those help made our life so much easier in this strange city. Everytime we met, we exchanged our different cultures and customs, which gave both sides so much surprise and fun. We created chances and expanded topics for knowing each other. Paul, an excellent painter, asked me and my husband as models, painting portraits for each of us. We keep those paintings as treasure forever.

Often, on some topics we could not understand each other, and fell into an awkward silence. No wonder, how could this not happen between us, who were from a totally different hemisphere? However, any conversation, hard or not, could be easily replaced by a relaxing topic concerning love, to our kids, family and people, which is the same language in the whole world. I thought our friendship would always stay strong.

However, it didn’t, at least, not always.

When David went to school, I got a job, my husband had his own business. We became more and more busy, those jobless and leisure times in our early days of immigration just passed. Plus, we moved, farther to Paul’s home. We could not see Paul and Marie that often anymore.

That summer, Paul scheduled a trip with my family, to Quebec City, and then to saint-sauveur. He planned with high expectations, me too, we were all excited. It was not bad. However, we were so different in all the details, eating, lodging, caring kids, even the way we enjoyed the landscapes. At the end, I could see from Paul’s face his disappointment. And from my side, during the trip, joyful and embarrassed moments,  relaxed and boring feelings, shifted all the time. 

Back home, we kept in touch by email and some planned visits, but I gradually felt a distance with them since then. 

One day, they came to my husband's store, bringing a big, nice plant as a gift, decorating the store. Marie worked hard to plant it into a big pot. At noon, we had lunch in the restaurant near my husband’s store. Weirdly, we were not talking as pleasantly as before, even though my husband and I were so grateful and happy, and they were glad too. 

One year later, we moved again, much farther from them, and I gave birth to my daughter.  After a long journey, they came for my baby. Marie weaved a beautiful baby blanket. During dinner, We had a really warm and cheerful talk, which made me think that I was wrong to feel the distance of our friendship.

Soon, my father passed away, I fell into sorrow. Being a musician, Paul composed a piano piece for my father, to express his condolences, which was so touching.

The following 5 or 6 years were not easy for Paul and Marie. They moved also, and encountered some difficulties running the homecare, then less income. The worse is, Marie was sick, had breast cancer, she got surgery successfully, but also had some other health problems.I was so sorry to hear that, and wish that I could help them. But in those years, this culture and society were still challenging me. What could I do but just say something comforting?

We still kept in touch, with some dinners, visits, but only once or twice a year. Each time we met, regardless of all kinds of problems that happened to them, they tried to keep the conversation in a happy air, stayed strong and elegant. But the atmosphere still changed, more silence, lacking common topics, even though we both tried to search. Distance feeling shew up again.

The last meeting was in their new rental apartment. I went there with my daughter, also after a long journey. The homecare was opening. They looked busy as usual. We talked about changes after our last meeting, some of which seemed complicated to each other. Rather than sharing, it was more like just noticing each other. 

A few months later, we talked by message, and they moved again. And then, no contact anymore, it was about 15 years after our first meeting at their home.

Maybe, it is easy to share our culture and customs by talking, but by not acting. And it is comfortable to share the happiness, but not the pain. I might’ve been self-completent and underestimated the cultural diversity. We both used to be lost, misunderstood, then tried to explain, respect, and lost, misunderstood again…Then I became cautious and warned, even though I still tried to resume and advance the friendship. Not to mention that, during those years, we both encountered all kinds of problems at work, family or financial issues. Finally it turned out that not only physically, but also mentally, we couldn’t keep close anymore.

Sometimes I believe there’s something wrong from my own side, and I believe they forgave me. But forgiveness might lead to giving up…

Friendship is hard to keep strong forever, even between childhood friends, even between people of the same culture, same races. But failing to keep it strong with Paul and Marie is the only one I felt regret, which gave me such a happy time in those hard years. 

Meanwhile, since then, I’m getting more careful when communicating with people from any different culture. I didn’t make any friends with other local people than Paul and Marie. Any chance coming, I back down, at least for now.

I always love this couple, appreciate what they’ve given to me, and value this precious experience in my life. Friends or not, I hope they are always going very well.
